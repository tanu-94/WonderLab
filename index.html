What is CT & AI in Class 3?

A simple, clear guide for teachers and parents: what these terms mean, why they matter, and how children use them in everyday life.

THE HUMAN HABIT

1. Computational Thinking (CT)

What it is: A step-by-step problem-solving method. It is the logical process of breaking down a large task, spotting repeating patterns, and organizing clear instructions so anyone (or any machine) can follow them without confusion[cite: 98].

What is the use of CT in real life?
  • In School: Solving word problems in Class 3 Math, decomposing numbers into hundreds and tens, and organizing tiffin boxes[cite: 7].
  • At Home: Packing school bags according to the timetable, following a recipe to make lemonade, or giving directions to reach home[cite: 8].
  • In Technology: It is the logical foundation engineers use before writing software[cite: 98, 99].
THE MACHINE APPLICATION

2. Artificial Intelligence (AI)

What it is: Technology that mimics human decision-making by recognizing patterns from past examples (data) to make predictions or choices, rather than following rigid, static rules[cite: 98].

What is the use of AI in daily life?
  • Voice Recognition: Recognizing your voice when asking a phone or smart speaker to play a song.
  • Map Navigation: Predicting the fastest route through city traffic.
  • Data Privacy & Security: Protecting parents' UPI payments, ATM PINs, and passwords from unauthorized access using cryptographic keys[cite: 104].

❓ Teacher & Parent Doubt Desk

Quick answers to common questions about CBSE Class 3 CT & AI.

Will there be written exams or marks?
No. CBSE strictly mandates 100% formative assessment through teacher observation journals and rubrics. No pen-and-paper marks or tests[cite: 8, 100].
What if our school has no computer lab?
Every single chapter is 100% unplugged. Lessons run on printed paper templates, chalkboards, and classroom games. Computers are completely optional[cite: 7, 28].
How does a cipher wheel connect to Math?
It teaches modular arithmetic (wrap-around addition), skip sequences, pattern recognition, and letter bisection (A–M and N–Z)[cite: 7, 102, 147].
